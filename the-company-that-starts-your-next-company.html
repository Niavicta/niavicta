The company that starts your next company.

The hardest part of starting a company is the scaffolding nobody hands you on day one. This is the thing we wished we had at the start.

Founder Diaries Chani Galgut 27 July 2026

Jasmine and I were prepping a demo this week, walking through project zero one more time, and Jasmine stopped in the middle of it and said "can you imagine if we had this when we just started?"

I said, "I know."

She kept going, standing there looking at it, and she said, can you imagine just launching a company, and starting out with this? I did not even think of that, that was genuinely my reaction, and then I tried to actually picture it, the two of us at the beginning with nianav OS already standing. It would have changed everything. This was the plan the whole time, and still is.

Then it got a little ridiculous, in the best way. "Imagine your mom says, I want to start a company, and you just go, here is your everything. Just do it."

I mean, yeah that could work. You declare your scope, and project zero asks you the questions the regulations pose, locks your answers, and derives the rules that apply to you. It builds the organisation those frameworks ask for, the roles and the people who hold them. Every procedure gets authored from its requirements.

This is the thing nobody hands you on your first day.

Because the part of starting a company that actually breaks you is the invisible part. Everyone talks about the idea, the pitch, the product. Nobody talks about the scaffolding underneath, the procedures, the record of who decided what and why, the structure that lets ten people work like one. You build it in the dark, usually after something has already gone wrong, and you rebuild it every single time you grow. I have watched founders spend their first two years assembling it by hand, and I have done it myself.

So we built the thing we wished we had.

That is who I picture at the starting line now. A first-time founder who cannot afford a quality hire yet. Somebody's mom who always wanted to start something and never knew where to begin.

You, a year from now, on the next idea, beginning from structure instead of from scratch.

That is the company we are building. The one that starts your next company.

It would have meant a great deal to me to have something like this when Jasmine and I started the company, and soon many will.
